# The Six Design-Maturity Pillars

KPMG measures design maturity across six weighted pillars. Pulse tags every coaching
turn against the pillar it touches, which is how the dashboard builds a Design Quality
(DQ) score passively — no surveys.

## Values
<!-- meta: framework=Values Pillar; pillar=values; phase=empathy; industry=generic -->
Values is the strategic foundation (weight 25%, the heaviest). It covers whether the
organisation is genuinely entrepreneurial, creative, and learning-oriented — whether design
is treated as a way of thinking or just a styling step at the end. Signals: teams that start
from user needs rather than internal assumptions, that treat being wrong early as learning,
and that protect time for exploration.

## Behavior
<!-- meta: framework=Behavior Pillar; pillar=behavior; phase=define; industry=generic -->
Behavior (weight 20%) is about how leaders and teams actually act: do they energise,
engage, and enable design practice, or merely endorse it in slides? It shows up in whether
people pressure-test their own assumptions, invite dissent, and make decisions with evidence
rather than seniority. Behavior is where stated values either become real or quietly die.

## Climate
<!-- meta: framework=Climate Pillar; pillar=climate; phase=ideate; industry=generic -->
Climate (weight 20%) is the operating environment: collaboration across silos, psychological
safety to raise a risky idea, and simplicity of process so good work is not strangled by
bureaucracy. A healthy climate is visible when cross-functional people co-create early and
when junior voices can challenge a direction without career risk.

## Process
<!-- meta: framework=Process Pillar; pillar=process; phase=ideate; industry=generic -->
Process (weight 10%) covers the disciplined rhythm of explore → ideate → create → evolve —
diverging widely before converging, and iterating rather than committing to the first idea.
Maturity here is not about following a rigid method; it is about not skipping the hard phases
(especially empathy and validation) when under delivery pressure.

## Resources
<!-- meta: framework=Resources Pillar; pillar=resources; phase=prototype; industry=generic -->
Resources (weight 10%) is whether the people, systems, and project structures exist to do
design well: skilled practitioners, tools, research access, and budget that does not punish
iteration. A common failure is asking for design outcomes while resourcing only delivery.

## Success
<!-- meta: framework=Success Pillar; pillar=success; phase=test; industry=generic -->
Success (weight 15%) is about impact and measurement — at the external (customer), enterprise
(business), and individual (capability) levels. Mature organisations validate outcomes with
evidence and feed results back into the next cycle, rather than declaring victory at launch.
